## What to look for

Foam rollers differ most in density, length and how well they keep their shape.

Medium density is the sensible starting point. Soft rollers squash under body weight, while hard or heavily ridged ones can be too intense for tired legs at the end of a day.

Length decides what you can do with it. A 30 cm roller is handy for calves and for travel, but for rolling across the upper back, or both legs at once, something around 45 cm is steadier.

Durability comes down to the foam. Low-grade open-cell foam flattens and dents within months, whereas moulded closed-cell foams such as EPP hold their form for years.

## Why this one

Blackroll is a German brand that built its name on a medium-hardness roller, and the Standard 45 is the long version of that original, at 45 cm long and 15 cm across. It is moulded from EPP, a closed-bead foam that is light, at about 240 g, shrugs off sweat, wipes clean and keeps its shape well with regular use.

The 45 cm length gives enough width to lie across for the upper back and to roll both calves or both hamstrings together, and it doubles as a balance and core tool if you want to add exercises later. When ordering, choose the Standard, medium-hardness option, as the listing also offers softer and harder grades.

## Who it suits

People who come off a shift with stiff calves, hamstrings and upper back and want ten minutes on the floor before bed. It also suits anyone new to rolling, because the medium grade is forgiving without being mushy.

## Who should skip it

If you mainly want a roller for travelling, the 30 cm version packs better. If you already find firm pressure uncomfortable, start with Blackroll's softer grade instead. And anyone with an injury or a medical condition should get advice from a GP or physiotherapist before rolling over it.
