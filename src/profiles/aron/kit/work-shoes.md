## What to look for

On a café or kitchen floor, what matters most is grip on wet, soapy or greasy tiles, and the only reliable way to compare shoes is certification. Occupational shoes without a toe cap are tested under EN ISO 20347, and the SRC mark means the sole passed both standard slip tests: ceramic tile with detergent, and steel with glycerine.

Look beyond the sole too. An upper you can wipe or wash stops spilt milk and coffee from soaking in, a cushioned footbed helps across a long shift, and a sole marked FO resists oil and fuel.

## Why this one

The DIAN Marsella comes from a Spanish manufacturer that has made occupational footwear since 1987 for hospitals, hotels and restaurants. It is certified to EN ISO 20347 at the SRC + O1 + FO level. In plain terms, that is the full slip-resistance rating, a closed heel, antistatic properties and energy absorption at the heel, plus an oil-resistant outsole.

The upper is a breathable microfibre that DIAN says can be washed, and the stitching between sole and upper is designed to survive hot-water cleaning. Inside there is a padded polyurethane foam footbed with an antibacterial finish. It comes in black for front-of-house and white for kitchens and healthcare.

## Who it suits

Baristas, waiting staff, kitchen porters and anyone who crosses wet floors all day and wants a plain, light shoe that fits a uniform policy without looking like a safety boot.

## Who should skip it

If your work involves heavy objects, kegs or roll cages, you need a safety shoe with a toe cap, certified under EN ISO 20345 instead. And if you have wide feet, try a pair on before committing, as fit is where opinions divide most.
