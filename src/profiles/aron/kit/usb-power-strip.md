## What to look for

Most homes end up with a tangle of chargers: a phone, earbuds, a watch, a tablet and a laptop, each with its own plug. A power strip with USB ports built in replaces most of those bricks, frees up sockets and keeps the cables in one place, whether that is a kitchen counter, a desk or a bedside table.

Check three things. The total USB wattage and whether the ports share it, because a laptop charging over USB-C needs far more power than a phone. The number of mains sockets, for everything that still needs a plug. And the protection: surge protection for your devices, overload protection for the strip itself, and a cable long enough to reach where you want it.

## Why this one

The VOOMY strip has five mains sockets and five USB ports, three USB-C and two USB-A. The USB ports share up to 100 W, and the two USB-A ports support 18 W quick charging for older phones and accessories. The strip is rated for up to 4,000 W across its sockets and cuts out if that is exceeded, and the USB side has surge protection.

A 2 m cable gives plenty of reach from a wall socket to a worktop or desk, a main switch turns everything off at once, and non-slip feet keep it in place. The maker offers a three-year warranty.

## Who it suits

Households that charge several phones and a laptop in the same spot, a small back office, or anyone whose bedside or kitchen counter has more chargers than sockets.

## Who should skip it

If your laptop needs a big charger, check the per-port ratings on the listing first: with other devices sharing the 100 W, it may charge slowly. If you only ever charge one phone, a single USB-C wall charger is simpler. And for expensive audio or TV equipment, a dedicated surge protector with a stated joule rating is the safer choice.
