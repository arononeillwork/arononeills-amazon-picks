## What to look for

Some people's work off the shop floor goes well beyond admin: editing photos and short videos for social media, designing menus and flyers, running large spreadsheets, or keeping a lot open at once. That is where a laptop's chip, memory and screen start to matter.

For that kind of work, look for plenty of memory, so the machine stays quick with several heavy apps open. A fast, roomy drive helps if you store photo and video files. A bright, accurate screen matters if you judge colours. And ports save you carrying adapters to plug in a camera card, a monitor or a projector.

## Why this one

This 14-inch MacBook Pro uses Apple's M5 Pro chip, here with a 15-core processor and 16-core graphics, alongside 24 GB of memory and a 1 TB drive. That is comfortably more than photo editing, video trimming and heavy multitasking need.

The 14.2-inch Liquid Retina XDR display runs at up to 120 Hz and reaches up to 1,000 nits sustained and 1,600 nits peak for HDR content, so it stays readable in bright rooms. Apple rates the battery at up to 22 hours of video playback, and MagSafe fast charging reaches about half in 30 minutes with the right adapter. The ports cover most needs without a hub: Thunderbolt, HDMI, an SD card slot and a headphone jack. This listing is the Space Black version with a Spanish keyboard.

## Who it suits

People who create content or handle large files for their business, and anyone who wants one laptop that will stay fast for many years, at home, on the train or at a supplier's office.

## Who should skip it

If the laptop is only for email, invoices, bookings and banking, the MacBook Neo or a MacBook Air does that for much less and weighs less. If you need Windows-only software, a Mac won't run it. And if you mostly work at a desk with a big monitor, a Mac mini or desktop may make more sense.
