## What to look for

Making espresso by hand means grinding fresh, weighing a dose, tamping it evenly, timing the shot and then steaming milk to the right temperature. That is a skill, and after a long day it is the last thing most people want to practise. If you want a proper milk coffee at home without the learning curve, look for a machine that automates the fiddly steps but still uses fresh beans and a real portafilter.

Three things matter. A built-in grinder with plenty of adjustment, because espresso is fussy about grind size. Automatic dosing and tamping, so every shot starts the same way. And automatic milk steaming with temperature and texture settings, including for plant milks.

## Why this one

The Oracle Jet grinds, doses and tamps straight into a 58 mm portafilter. Its conical burr grinder has 45 settings, and a touchscreen offers preset drinks and lets you save your own.

The Auto MilQ wand textures milk hands-free, with settings for dairy, soy, almond and oat milk, temperatures from 40 to 75 °C and eight texture levels, and it can queue the milk to start as soon as the shot has finished. Sage's ThermoJet heating system gets it ready quickly. The bean hopper holds 340 g, the water tank 2.3 litres, and a knock box comes in the box.

## Who it suits

Coffee drinkers who want a flat white or cappuccino at home every day, and households where more than one person makes the coffee: the automation keeps the results consistent whoever presses the button.

## Who should skip it

If you only drink black coffee, or you enjoy the craft of manual espresso, a separate grinder and a simpler machine cost less and teach you more. Measure the space under your wall cupboards before ordering. And if you want a machine you hardly think about, a fully automatic bean-to-cup model needs less hands-on cleaning.
