## What to look for

On a long shift, a proper lunch from home beats whatever is left in the fridge at work. A good lunch box needs to hold enough for a real meal, keep different foods apart, survive being thrown in a bag every day and be quick to clean.

Material is the first choice. Plastic is light and often microwave-safe, but it stains and holds smells. Glass can go in the microwave but is heavy and breakable. Stainless steel is tough, doesn't stain or hold odours and lasts for years, but it can't go in the microwave. Then check the size, the number of compartments and whether it can go in the dishwasher.

## Why this one

This Everusely bento box is made of 304 stainless steel, the grade widely used for food containers, which resists rust, stains and smells. It holds 0.8 litres across three compartments: one large section for a main and two smaller ones for sides or snacks.

A separate leakproof pot keeps dressings and sauces away from the rest until you're ready to eat. The box goes in the dishwasher and the freezer, so you can batch-cook and freeze portions, and it's light enough to carry every day. This listing is the lavender colour.

## Who it suits

People who take salads, sandwiches, rice dishes, fruit and snacks to work, those who want to cut down on plastic, and anyone whose workplace has a plate and a microwave for reheating.

## Who should skip it

If you want to heat your lunch in the container itself, choose a glass or microwave-safe plastic box instead: metal must never go in a microwave. If you often bring soups, stews or very saucy dishes, get a container with sealed, leakproof compartments or a flask. And if you need food kept hot or cold for hours, look at an insulated lunch box.
