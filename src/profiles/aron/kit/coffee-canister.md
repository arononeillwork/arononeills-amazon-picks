## What to look for

Roasted coffee starts losing its aroma as soon as the bag is opened. Oxygen does most of the damage, helped along by light, heat and moisture. A good storage jar deals with the first and keeps the others out.

Look for a seal that removes air rather than just closing a lid on it, a size that matches how much coffee you buy, and a container that is easy to keep clean, because the oils coffee leaves behind go stale too.

## Why this one

The Atmos has a small vacuum pump built into its lid. You twist the lid back and forth, each turn draws out a little more air, and an indicator on top shows a green ring once the canister is properly sealed. Fellow says this keeps beans fresh for noticeably longer than an ordinary airtight jar.

The 1.2-litre size holds about 450 g of light or medium roast beans, roughly one bag of specialty coffee or a week or two of daily espresso. Darker roasts take up more room, so a full bag of those may not quite fit. The glass body washes like any jar, and the canister works just as well for loose tea, spices or ground coffee.

## Who it suits

Anyone who buys freshly roasted specialty beans and gets through a bag every couple of weeks, especially alongside a home grinder or an espresso machine with a built-in grinder, where fresh beans make the most difference in the cup.

## Who should skip it

If you buy pre-ground supermarket coffee in large packs, a simple airtight tin does most of the job for less. If your coffee lives on an open shelf in daylight, choose one of the opaque versions of the Atmos rather than clear glass. And if you want the quickest possible routine, remember the twisting adds a few seconds every time you close it.
