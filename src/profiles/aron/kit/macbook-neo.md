## What to look for

If most of your working day happens on your feet, the laptop only has to handle the admin around it: rotas, invoices, supplier orders, email and the occasional video call. That doesn't need a powerful machine. It needs a good screen, a battery that lasts, a comfortable keyboard and a weight you won't mind carrying.

Check the storage too. Documents, photos of receipts and the odd spreadsheet add up over the years, and a larger drive saves you managing space later. And if you pay for things with your phone and log in to banking and supplier sites, a fingerprint reader makes the daily sign-ins much quicker.

## Why this one

The MacBook Neo is Apple's entry-level laptop, launched in 2026. It runs on the A18 Pro, the chip Apple first used in the iPhone 16 Pro, which is quick for everyday office work and very frugal with power: Apple rates the battery at up to 16 hours.

The 13-inch Liquid Retina display is sharp and bright, the 1080p camera is good enough for video calls, and at about 1.2 kg it slips easily into a work bag. This version has 512 GB of storage and Touch ID, which the cheaper version leaves out. It comes with a Spanish keyboard, two USB-C ports and a headphone jack, in an enclosure made largely from recycled aluminium.

## Who it suits

Anyone who needs a dependable, light laptop for small-business admin and personal use, especially if you already use an iPhone and want messages, photos and passwords to move between them without effort.

## Who should skip it

If you keep dozens of browser tabs, a large spreadsheet and a video call open at once, or edit photos and video, the 8 GB of memory will hold you back: a MacBook Air or MacBook Pro suits you better. If you need several ports, or work in dim light and rely on a backlit keyboard, look elsewhere. And if your accounting software only runs on Windows, a Mac won't run it.
