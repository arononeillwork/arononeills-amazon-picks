## What to look for

A standing mat earns its place on the hard tiles behind a bar or counter, where you shift your weight from foot to foot for hours. Three things matter.

First, the cushioning. Dense foam or gel should give a little underfoot without feeling like a mattress, because a very soft mat makes you work harder to stay balanced.

Second, bevelled edges that slope down to the floor, so a trolley wheel, a mop or a tired foot rolls over the border instead of catching it.

Third, a closed, wipe-clean surface. A café floor sees milk, coffee grounds, water and the occasional splash of oil, and none of it should soak in.

## Why this one

The Sky Solutions mat is 19 mm of high-density polyurethane foam with a textured diamond pattern on top. The maker describes the surface as water- and stain-resistant, and spills come off with a damp cloth. The edges are bevelled all the way round and the underside is non-slip, which matters on wet tiles.

The 50 x 99 cm size covers the working zone in front of an espresso machine or a prep bench without spreading into walkways, and longer runner sizes are sold if your station is bigger. It is light enough to lift and lean against a wall while you mop underneath at closing time.

## Who it suits

Anyone who spends a shift in roughly the same spot: baristas, bar staff, shop counter workers, hairdressers, or someone with a standing desk at home.

## Who should skip it

If you work next to a fryer or a grill line where hot fat lands on the floor, look for a nitrile rubber kitchen mat with drainage holes instead; foam is the wrong material there. And if your job keeps you moving around a large area rather than standing at one station, good shoes and insoles will do more for you than a mat.
