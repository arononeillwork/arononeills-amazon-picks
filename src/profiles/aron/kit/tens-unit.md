## What it is

A TENS unit is a small battery-powered device that sends a mild electrical current through sticky pads placed on the skin. You control the intensity and the pattern. Manufacturers describe the sensation as tingling or buzzing, and sell them for personal comfort at home. EMS is related but stronger: it makes the muscle under the pads contract and relax.

## What to look for

If you're choosing one, these are the things that matter day to day:

- **Two channels**, so you can place pads on both calves, or both sides of the lower back, at the same time.
- **Small intensity steps**, so you can start barely above nothing and build up slowly.
- **Pads that are easy to replace.** The gel loses its stick with use, so you will be buying more.
- **More than one kind of stimulation**, so you can find a setting whose feel you like.

## Who it suits

People who finish a long standing shift with tired calves or a stiff back and want to try electrical stimulation at home, sitting down, before spending more on an established brand.

## Who should skip it

Anyone the "Before you buy" note on this page applies to, in particular anyone with a pacemaker or other implanted device, a heart condition or epilepsy, or who is pregnant, should ask a doctor first. Skip it too if you dislike the sensation of the current, or if you would rather pay more for a unit from a medical brand with a long track record.
