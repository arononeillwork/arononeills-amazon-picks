## What to look for

Running a small business means a steady trickle of admin: invoices, supplier orders, rotas, bookkeeping, tax forms and email. None of it needs a powerful machine, but a few things make it easier.

Sixteen gigabytes of memory keeps a browser full of tabs, a spreadsheet and a video call running smoothly. A 512 GB solid-state drive or larger leaves room for years of documents. Battery life and weight matter if the laptop travels between home, the business and the gestor's office.

Laptops sold in Spain come with a Spanish keyboard, which adds the ñ key and moves some symbols, such as @, to different keys. You can switch the layout in settings, but the printed labels stay Spanish.

## Why these three

The Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 3 is the no-frills option: a 15.3-inch screen in a taller 16:10 shape that shows more rows of a spreadsheet, an Intel Core i5 processor and Windows 11 Home included. It weighs about 1.6 kg and its battery is modest, so it suits a desk more than a daily bag.

The ASUS Zenbook A14 is built for carrying. It weighs under a kilogram, has a sharp OLED screen, and its Snapdragon chip is very frugal with power. The trade-off is Windows on Arm, which runs most modern apps well but can trip up older specialist software.

The MacBook Air 13 with the M5 chip has no fan, so it is completely silent, and it weighs about 1.23 kg. If your phone is an iPhone, moving text, photos and passwords between the two is effortless.

## Who should skip these

If your accounting or tax software only runs on Windows, rule out the MacBook and check with the software's maker before choosing the Zenbook. Anyone who edits video or large photo libraries will want more power than the Lenovo offers.
