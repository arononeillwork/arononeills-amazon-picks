## What to look for

A busy bar, a kitchen at full service or a café with the grinder and steam wand going all morning can be louder than people realise, and hours of it add up. Ordinary foam earplugs cut the noise but muffle everything, which is no use when you need to hear an order, a colleague or the till.

For that kind of setting, look for high-fidelity earplugs: they lower all frequencies by a similar amount, so voices and music sound like themselves, only quieter. Check the rated noise reduction, usually given as an SNR figure in decibels. Comfort matters over a long shift, so several tip sizes help, and reusable plugs you can wash are cheaper to live with than disposable ones.

## Why this one

The Loop Experience 2 is the concert and busy-venue model in Loop's range. Loop rates it at 17 dB SNR and says it qualifies as certified hearing protection. The acoustic channel inside is designed to keep sound clear, so conversation stays easy to follow while the overall volume drops.

It comes with four sizes of silicone ear tips, from extra small to large, with the medium pre-fitted, so most ears can find a secure seal. You push the plugs in and twist to lock them in place. They're reusable and easy to clean, and a small keychain case keeps the pair together in a pocket or on your keys.

## Who it suits

Bar, café and kitchen staff who work around constant noise and still need to talk to customers, anyone with a loud commute, and people who go to concerts or matches and want to leave without ringing ears.

## Who should skip it

If you want earplugs for sleeping, choose a model made for sleep with a higher rating, such as Loop's own Quiet range. If you work around genuinely loud machinery, use the hearing protection your employer provides for that noise level. And if you already find in-ear fits uncomfortable, over-ear protection may suit you better.
