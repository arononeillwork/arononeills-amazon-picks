## What to look for

Behind a hot counter in a Spanish summer, a bottle that keeps water cold matters more than one that looks good.

Double-wall stainless steel with a vacuum between the walls is the construction that does it; single-wall steel or plastic warms up within an hour or two. Look for a stated cold time of at least 12 hours, since a shift plus the journey home easily runs that long. The lid needs to be properly leakproof, not just splash-resistant, if the bottle shares a bag with a laptop. Finally, check the diameter: around 7 cm fits most car cup holders and backpack side pockets.

## Why this one

Hydro Flask's 709 ml Standard Mouth bottle is double-wall 18/8 stainless steel with vacuum insulation, which the maker rates at up to 24 hours cold and 12 hours hot. The Flex Cap screws down to a leakproof seal and has a flexible strap for carrying it on a finger or clipping it to a bag.

The body is about 7.2 cm across, so it slots into most car cup holders and the side pockets of commuter backpacks, and the outside stays dry rather than sweating onto the counter. The steel does not hold on to flavours, and Hydro Flask lists the bottle as dishwasher safe.

## Who it suits

Anyone who works a long shift in a warm room, or drives between jobs, and wants cold water on hand all day without buying single-use bottles. The 621 ml version is a few centimetres shorter if your cup holder sits under a low dashboard lip.

## Who should skip it

If you mostly drink hot coffee on the move, a travel mug with a one-handed lid will be easier. If you like plenty of ice, or want to clean the inside by hand, choose a wide-mouth model instead. And if your bottle gets knocked off a counter every week, a bare steel finish hides the damage better than powder coat.
