## What to look for

Graduated compression means the sock is tightest at the ankle and eases off towards the knee. Manufacturers state the pressure in millimetres of mercury, mmHg, and that number is the first thing to check. Light everyday socks sit around 15-20 mmHg. The 18-22 mmHg band is a common step up for people who stand for long stretches or fly often. Anything from 20-30 mmHg upwards is firmer and usually chosen with a pharmacist's advice.

A proper pair is sized by measuring the ankle, and sometimes the calf, rather than by shoe size alone, because the pressure is only what the label says if the fit is right. For a shift in a warm room you also want a yarn that copes with sweat and odour.

## Why this one

The Relaxsan 830 is an Italian-made, unisex knee-high sock with a stated 18-22 mmHg of graduated compression at the ankle. Relaxsan's size chart pairs shoe size with an ankle circumference range, so measuring first thing in the morning, before your feet have been on the go, gets you onto the right size.

The yarn includes X-Static silver fibre, which the maker says helps with odour and temperature, and the heel and toe are reinforced for wear. In black it looks like an ordinary work sock under trousers, which suits a front-of-house job.

## Who it suits

People who stand behind a counter, bar or shop till all day and notice heavy, tired legs by the evening, and anyone who wants one pair that also works on the long flight home.

## Who should skip it

Anyone with a circulation problem, diabetes-related nerve changes or a skin condition on the legs should talk to a GP or pharmacist first, as set out in the health note. And if you already know you need firmer compression, this is not a substitute for a fitted medical stocking from a pharmacy.
