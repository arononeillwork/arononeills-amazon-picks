## What to look for

Most massage guns are sold on power: how hard they hit and how deep they reach. After a long shift on your feet, many people want something gentler, a few minutes on tight calves and shoulders that helps them switch off rather than a sports-recovery workout.

For that kind of use, look for several speed settings so you can start slowly, a grip that lets you reach your own back and shoulders without straining your wrist, and enough battery for a week of short evening sessions. Guidance helps too: most people are unsure where to hold a massage gun, and for how long.

## Why this one

The Theragun Sense is Therabody's relaxation-focused model. It has five speeds and a 12 mm stroke, and the brand's triangle-shaped handle gives you several ways to hold it, which makes your own back and shoulders easier to reach.

What sets it apart is the guidance. A screen on the device shows where to place it, for how long and how firmly, with four routines built in and room to save five more from Therabody's app over Bluetooth. A heart-rate sensor in the handle is used for guided breathing exercises, which Therabody pairs with the massage routines for winding down in the evening. The battery is rated for about two hours, so a week of ten-minute sessions fits comfortably on one charge, and it comes with a dampener head for sensitive areas and a standard ball for everything else.

## Who it suits

People who finish a long standing shift feeling wound up as well as sore, and who want a simple routine to follow on the sofa rather than working out technique for themselves. It also suits anyone new to massage guns who finds the stronger models too intense.

## Who should skip it

Anyone in the groups listed in the health note on this page should speak to a doctor first. If you want deep pressure for large, muscular legs, or a wide choice of attachments, a more powerful model suits you better. And if you would never use the screen, the app or the breathing routines, a simpler massage gun does the massage part for less.
