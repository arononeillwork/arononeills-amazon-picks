## What to look for

The insole that comes with most work shoes is a thin sheet of foam. A replacement for all-day standing should do two jobs: hold the arch, so the foot is not sinking flat into the shoe by mid-afternoon, and cushion the heel, which carries most of your weight on a hard floor.

A trim-to-fit outline matters because work shoes vary in shape, and an insole that rides up at the toe or slides at the heel will rub. Check the thickness too. A cushioned insole takes up room, so it works best in shoes with a removable footbed.

## Why this one

Scholl designed the GelActiv Work range for people who stand or walk on hard surfaces through a shift. It pairs firmer gel under the arch and heel with softer gel for cushioning, a combination Scholl calls GelWave, and the top layer is made to absorb sweat and keep odour down.

The outline has printed trim lines, so you either cut along your size or trace round the shoe's original insole. There are separate versions for men's sizes 40 to 46.5 and women's 35.5 to 40.5, and Scholl suggests replacing a pair after about six months of daily use.

## Who it suits

People with neutral or higher arches who mainly want more cushioning under the heel and forefoot during a long shift, in trainers, work shoes or boots with removable insoles. It is also an easy upgrade for a certified slip-resistant work shoe, which often ships with a basic footbed.

## Who should skip it

If you have flat feet, roll inwards noticeably when you walk, or have been told you need firm arch support, a structured insole such as a medium-arch Superfeet model will hold the foot better, and a podiatrist can advise on custom orthotics. Skip it too for close-fitting shoes with fixed insoles, where there is no room for the extra thickness.
