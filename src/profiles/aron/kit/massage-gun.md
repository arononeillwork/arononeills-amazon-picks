## What to look for

A massage gun for after a shift should be easy to live with rather than as powerful as possible.

Several speed settings let you start gently on tired calves and go firmer on thighs or shoulders. Noise matters if you share walls or use it while someone else is watching television, so a stated decibel figure is useful. Battery life should comfortably cover a week of short evening sessions: ten or fifteen minutes a night adds up to less than two hours. And weight decides whether you can reach your own shoulders and back without your wrist tiring.

## Why this one

The Hypervolt Go 3 is the compact model in Hyperice's current range. It has five speeds, from 1,500 to 2,500 percussions per minute, and Hyperice quotes about 55 dB in use thanks to what it calls QuietGlide, which is towards the quiet end for this kind of device.

The battery is rated for around four hours and charges over USB-C, so a week of evenings sits well within one charge and it shares a cable with your phone. At about 0.73 kg with an angled handle, it is light enough to use one-handed. It comes with a flat head for general use, a wedge head for narrower areas, and a carry case, and Hyperice pitches it as suitable for cabin bags.

## Who it suits

People who finish a long standing shift with tight calves and lower legs and want a few minutes of massage while they sit down, without the bulk or noise of a full-size gun. It also packs easily for trips back home.

## Who should skip it

Anyone in the groups listed in the health note should speak to a doctor first. If you want very deep pressure for large, muscular legs, or a wider choice of attachments, a full-size gun gives you more at the cost of weight. And if you mainly want to work the soles of your feet, a firm massage ball does that job with less fuss.
