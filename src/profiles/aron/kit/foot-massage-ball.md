## What to look for

After a day standing, the soles and arches are often the tightest part of the body, and a small, firm tool you can roll under your foot while sitting down is the simplest way to work them.

Firmness matters. A ball that squashes flat under body weight does little, while a very hard one can be too much for tender feet at first. A diameter of around 6 to 8 cm fits the arch well. Smooth or textured is a personal choice: smooth surfaces spread the pressure evenly, spiky ones feel more intense. A set with different shapes or densities lets you vary the pressure and reach the heel, arch and calf with one purchase.

## Why this one

The Blackbox Mini puts three of Blackroll's small tools in one box, all moulded from the same EPP foam as the brand's full-size rollers.

The Ball 08 is an 8 cm sphere for pressing into the arch and the ball of the foot. The Duoball 08 is a peanut shape, 16 cm long, that sits either side of the Achilles tendon or runs along the calf. The Mini is a 15 cm roller about 5 cm across, a good size for rolling the whole length of the sole under a desk or in front of the television.

The foam is odour-free, wipes clean and keeps its shape, and the pieces are small enough to live in a work bag or suitcase.

## Who it suits

Anyone who finishes a shift with tired, tight feet and wants a few minutes of rolling before bed without buying a machine. The mix of shapes means one set covers the feet and lower legs.

## Who should skip it

If your feet are very sensitive, start with a softer ball or a set with graded densities, as these are all medium-firm. If you want something textured for a more intense feel, a spiky rubber ball is smaller and sharper. And anyone with a foot injury, diabetes-related foot problems or ongoing heel pain should get a GP or podiatrist's view before rolling on it.
